How Much Do Uber Drivers Earn in London? (2026 Guide)

A straight answer to the question every new driver asks: what you can expect to earn driving for Uber in London, what comes out of it, and what makes the biggest difference to your take-home pay.

Last reviewed: November 2026 9 min read Written by the Virtual Car Hire team

The short answer

Typical London earnings in 2026

Most driver guides and surveys put London Uber earnings at roughly £15 to £24 an hour before costs, and around £10 to £17 an hour after costs such as car hire, fuel and the Congestion Charge. These are rough ranges from industry guides and driver surveys, not official figures. Your own result depends on when you drive, which categories you can take, and what your car costs to run.

The spread is wide because two drivers doing the same hours can have very different weeks. A driver who works Friday and Saturday nights, takes early airport runs and drives an economical car will usually keep far more than one who drives quiet weekday afternoons in a thirsty car.

Where the money goes

What a passenger pays is not what you keep. In London, a share of each fare goes to Uber as a service fee. Many guides still quote around 25%, but Uber has moved to more variable fees in its newer UK terms, so check the fare breakdown in your driver app for the real figure on each trip.

Change one thing and the result moves a lot. Swap two quiet weekday shifts for Friday and Saturday nights and the fares figure often rises. Switch to an MG5 EV and fuel drops, though the car costs less to hire too. This is why tracking your own numbers for a few weeks matters more than any average.

Minimum earnings and holiday pay

Since the 2021 Supreme Court ruling, Uber treats UK drivers as workers while they are on a trip. That brings some protections:

  • Minimum earnings guarantee: Uber says it guarantees at least the National Living Wage for time spent on trips, from accepting a request to completing it, checked each week after vehicle expenses. Time waiting between trips is not covered.
  • Holiday pay: Uber pays an extra 12.07% of your earnings as holiday pay.
  • Pension: eligible drivers can join a pension scheme with contributions from Uber.

The National Living Wage changes every April. Check the current rate on gov.uk and Uber's own driver pages for how the guarantee is calculated.

How to earn more

  • Drive the busy hours. Weekend nights, Monday mornings and late evenings when the Tube shuts usually pay best.
  • Use airports wisely. Early-morning Heathrow runs can be long, well-paid trips. Read our airport transfers guide before you join a queue.
  • Unlock better categories. Comfort and Exec fares pay more per mile. See our guide to stepping up to Exec and executive cars like the Mercedes EQE.
  • Cut running costs. An electric car saves on fuel and pays less Congestion Charge on Auto Pay. Our Congestion Charge guide shows the numbers.
  • Keep your receipts. Car hire, fuel and phone costs can be claimed against tax. See our tax guide.
  • Run more than one app. Many drivers use Uber and Bolt together to cut waiting time.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an Uber driver earn in London per hour?

Most guides put it at roughly £15 to £24 an hour before costs and around £10 to £17 after costs. It varies a lot with the hours you drive, the categories you can take and what your car costs to run.

How much does Uber take from drivers in London?

A service fee is taken from each fare. Many guides still quote around 25%, but Uber now uses more variable fees in some UK terms, so check the fare breakdown in your driver app for the actual amount.

Do Uber drivers get paid holiday?

Yes. Uber pays UK drivers an extra 12.07% of their earnings as holiday pay.

Is renting a PCO car worth it?
