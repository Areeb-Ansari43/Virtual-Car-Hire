PCO Driver Guides for London and UK Drivers

Everything you need to know about getting your TfL PCO licence, choosing Uber & Bolt eligible cars, stepping up to Uber Exec, navigating ULEZ and Congestion Charges, and maximizing tax deductions in London.

What is a PCO driver?

A PCO driver is a private hire driver licensed by Transport for London (TfL). The name comes from the Public Carriage Office, which used to issue the licences. PCO drivers carry passengers booked through apps such as Uber, Bolt and Ola, or through minicab and chauffeur firms. Read how to get a PCO licence in London.

What is a PCO car?

A PCO car is a vehicle licensed by TfL as a private hire vehicle. It must pass a TfL inspection, show PHV licence discs and carry Hire and Reward insurance. Virtual Car Hire rents TfL licensed PCO cars from £200 a week with insurance included.

PCO car requirements

Cars licensed by TfL as private hire vehicles for the first time must be zero emission capable, which means fully electric or a plug-in hybrid. Age limits and inspections apply too. See TfL private hire vehicle rules and the Uber car list for 2026.
