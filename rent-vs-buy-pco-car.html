Rent, Rent-to-Buy or Buy a PCO Car? (2026)

Every PCO driver has to decide how to get a car. Renting, rent-to-buy and buying outright all work, but they suit different drivers. Here is how they compare in 2026.

Last reviewed: November 2026 8 min read Written by the Virtual Car Hire team

The three options

Option 1

Renting

A fixed weekly price, usually with insurance, servicing and MOT included. Hand the car back when your minimum term ends. No big upfront cost.

Option 2

Rent-to-buy

Weekly payments over a longer term, after which you own the car or pay a final amount. Insurance and maintenance may or may not be included.

Option 3

Buying

Pay for a car outright or with finance, then license and insure it yourself. You keep the car, but also every cost and risk.

How TfL's vehicle rules affect buying

If you buy a car to use for private hire in London, it needs its own TfL private hire vehicle licence, and the rules have tightened:

  • Since 1 January 2023, any car licensed as a PHV for the first time must be zero emission capable (ZEC) and meet Euro 6. In practice that means a fully electric car or a plug-in hybrid that qualifies.
  • TfL guidance says licensed vehicles must be no older than 10 years when they are relicensed, so an older used car may only have a few working years left.
  • In October 2026 TfL consulted on requiring all newly licensed PHVs to be fully zero emission from 2028. That is a proposal for now, but it matters if you are buying a car to keep.

Our TfL vehicle rules guide explains these in more detail. Renting a car that is already TfL licensed takes these questions off your plate.

The costs of owning that people forget

  • Depreciation. Cars lose value fastest when driven high mileage, which private hire always is.
  • Insurance. Private hire cover in London often runs to thousands a year. See our insurance guide.
  • Downtime. A week off the road for repairs is a week with no income.
  • Vehicle licensing and MOTs. PHVs need a TfL vehicle licence and regular inspections.

Which suits you?

Renting suits new drivers, anyone testing whether private hire is for them, drivers who want to try Comfort or Exec categories, and anyone who would rather have one predictable weekly cost.

Buying suits experienced, full-time drivers with savings, who are comfortable managing insurance, repairs and licensing, and who plan to keep driving for years.

Rent-to-buy sits in between, but read the contract carefully: the total you pay over the term, who covers repairs, mileage limits, and what happens if you need to stop early.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to rent or buy a PCO car?

It depends on how long you will drive and how much risk you want. Renting has no big upfront cost and includes insurance and servicing; buying can work out cheaper over several years if nothing goes wrong.

Can I buy any used car and use it for Uber in London?

No. A car licensed as a PHV for the first time must be zero emission capable and meet Euro 6, and there are age limits on relicensing.

What is rent-to-buy?

You pay weekly over a longer contract and own the car, or can buy it, at the end. Check the total cost and who pays for repairs before you sign.

How long is the minimum rental term?

With Virtual Car Hire, most cars have a 6-week minimum and some have 8 weeks.
